## Move one · write the complaint down

Before enabling anything, put the problem into one sentence. "Slow when I have a browser and a virtual
machine open." "Hot underneath during video calls." "The connection allowance vanishes and I cannot say
where." Each of those sentences points at a different module, and the ones it does not point at should stay
dark for now. A strip built without a question on it becomes decoration within a fortnight.

## Move two · processor load goes up on its own

Enable it and nothing else. Seven presentations are available, so pick according to intent: the per-core
layout when you are hunting a single runaway process, a bare figure when you only want to know whether the
machine is busy. Watch it for an afternoon before adding company. Most people discover their idle number is
higher than they assumed, and that alone reframes the original complaint.

## Move three · memory joins underneath

Four presentations again, and the choice that matters is whether paging traffic and swap consumption appear
alongside the headline figure. They should. A machine deep in swap behaves exactly like a machine short of
processing power, and without those two numbers on display the two conditions are indistinguishable from the
keyboard.

## Move four · thermals and airflow arrive together

Never one without the other. Warmth on its own tells you little, since silicon is designed to run hot; warmth
with the fans still idling means something different from warmth with the fans at maximum. Both modules let
you drop sensors individually, and you should, because a modern Mac reports readings from components you have
no name for and no interest in.

## Move five · point networking at one interface

Turn the module on, then immediately hide everything that is not the connection carrying your traffic.
What remains shows movement in both directions, running totals, peak rates and addresses. On a metered or
tethered link this is the module that pays for the whole application, because background transfers are
invisible by design and enormous by habit.

## Move six · trim the disks, then take the clock

Storage comes last of the diagnostic modules and needs the same pruning treatment: keep the volumes you could
describe to somebody else, hide the rest. Only once those five are settled is it worth replacing the stock
clock with the richer one and its multiple cities, and hanging the forecast beside it. Cosmetics after
instruments, in that order.

## Where the strip stops and the panel starts

Every module has two faces. The strip is a summary sized for peripheral vision; clicking one opens a panel
carrying history, totals, per-core detail, addresses and sensor lists in full. Confusing the two is the most
common source of "the number looks wrong" — the summary is rounded and current, the panel is complete.

## Four habits worth keeping

Take a baseline while the machine is idle, because every later reading is only meaningful next to it. Order
the modules to match how your eye travels. Rebuild the whole strip after a month, dropping whatever you never
look at. And run a current build — this is 7.20, and advice written against much older lines describes
different behaviour. Do those and download istat menus for mac stays a five-minute job with a permanent
payoff.
